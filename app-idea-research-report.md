# App Idea Research Report

*Overnight research run, November 1, 2026. Ten research agents, roughly 270 web searches.*

---

## Read-aloud summary

Here is the bottom line. None of the ideas we found passed as a "cheap Zoom beats Webex" opportunity. Every niche we checked already had several cheap or free competitors, and new AI-built clones keep arriving every month. That pattern is the main finding of the night.

The research ran in two rounds. In the first round, five agents looked for openings in five places: price hikes and acquisitions, one-star reviews, Reddit and forum requests, product shutdowns and new laws, and areas that use your Zoom and real estate background. They found about forty candidates. In the second round, five more agents tried to rule out the top five. All five failed the test. Here are the scores, out of ten.

The Zoom webinar add-on scored four. Zoom's own Webinars Plus already does most of it, and Zoom keeps shipping new features. One narrow gap is left: checking and repairing webinar data that syncs into HubSpot and Marketo.

Membership software to replace WildApricot scored four. WildApricot keeps raising prices, but free and cheap tools like Heylo, Zeffy, Join It and Raklet are already winning over the people who leave.

PDF accessibility for small governments scored four. The new federal deadline is real, but older documents are exempt, AI tools already charge as little as thirty cents a page, and the big government website companies already include this service.

Back-office software for small real estate brokerages scored three. Every major vendor added AI document review in 2026, and many brokers get SkySlope free through their listing service.

Software for homeowner associations run by volunteer boards scored three. The leader, PayHOA, is cheap, well liked, funded, and profitable.

So what does this mean? AI coding made software cheap to build for everyone. A niche with paying customers and a visible complaint gets ten clones within months. Being cheaper and simpler is now the starting point, not an advantage. What clones can't copy is distribution and trust: relationships, channels, and knowledge from inside an industry.

Two angles came close, and both use something you have that a stranger with AI tools doesn't.

The first is the Zoom webinar data checker. It's a small tool for marketing teams that finds and fixes webinar sign-ups and attendees that never made it into HubSpot, Salesforce or Marketo. Your Zoom background gives you credibility and knowledge of how Zoom's systems connect. It scored about four, with a clear test.

The second is resale paperwork for volunteer-run homeowner associations. When a home sells, agents and title companies need documents from the association, and volunteer boards are often slow to produce them. The buyer's side pays the fee, so the association pays nothing. Your agent network is the way in. It scored about five.

My recommendation: don't build anything yet. Spend the next two weeks on two cheap tests. First, ask thirty of your agent contacts whether a self-managed association has delayed one of their closings in the past year. Second, ask a few former Zoom colleagues and webinar marketers how often webinar data goes missing in their CRM. If either test comes back strong, we build a small version. If both come back weak, the best source of your next idea is your own frustrations from work, not more web searching.

## How the research ran

**Round 1: finding candidates.** Five agents worked in parallel, each with a different source:
1. Price hikes and acquisitions, 2024–2026
2. Review-site complaint mining in software for small organizations
3. Reddit and community requests (Reddit itself was blocked; other forums were used instead)
4. Product shutdowns, stranded users, and new regulations that require software
5. Ideas that use the founder's Zoom background and real estate network

**Round 2: trying to kill the top 5.** One agent per idea, each told to find reasons it fails.

Network limits: reddit.com, devforum.zoom.us, ada.gov and some vendor sites were blocked in this session. Several claims therefore rely on search snippets or third-party aggregators. Many pricing sources are competitors' own blogs.

## What the research found overall

1. **Being cheaper is no longer an advantage.** In almost every category, at least one of the four research agents independently found AI-built clones, often lookalike sites on pages.dev or vercel.app, already competing.
2. **Incumbents are rarely hated by the people who buy them.** Review scores are mostly 4+ stars. The anger shows up as complaints about price increases, or comes from people who have to use software they didn't choose (residents, parents, ticket buyers).
3. **Platforms build features in quickly.** Zoom, Jobber, BoldTrail and SkySlope all shipped the feature we were evaluating during 2025–2026.
4. **What clones can't copy:** distribution channels (associations, federations, MLSs, marketplaces), trust, and knowledge from inside an industry. That's where the founder's advantage lies, not in coding speed.

## Recommended next steps (2 weeks, no code)

**Test A: resale document service for HOAs (uses the agent network)**
- Ask 30 agents: "In the past year, did a self-managed HOA delay resale or estoppel documents on one of your closings?" Continue if 30% or more say yes.
- Ask 3 title companies how they get documents from self-managed HOAs today and what it costs them.

**Test B: Zoom webinar data checker (uses the Zoom background)**
- Write a free audit script and run it on 10 real accounts to measure records that are in Zoom but missing from the CRM. Stop if under 2%, or if the fixes are all one-time setting changes.
- Interview 20 marketing-ops people, recruited from 1–3 star reviewers of the Zoom app on the HubSpot marketplace. Continue only with 5 or more paid pre-orders at ~$199/mo.
- Ask former Zoom colleagues whether HubSpot and Marketo support for Webinars Plus and Events is on Zoom's roadmap.

**If both tests fail:** list 10 frustrations from your own work, things you or your colleagues did by hand or complained about, and run the same kill test on each. Ideas that come from knowledge only you have are the ones clones can't reach.
