# Competitor Landscape: Community Platforms for Accountability Communities

**Prepared:** 2026-10-16. **Scope:** chat/community platforms, fitness/running/pickleball apps, and accountability-specific tools that a "Discord for accountability communities" product (real-time chat, live audio/video, paid memberships, native check-ins/streaks/challenges/stakes) would compete with or be compared to.

**Method and caveats.** All prices and product facts below were checked via web search in October 2026, preferring official help centers, press releases and SEC filings, then trade press, then third-party review blogs. Direct fetches of several official pricing pages (skool.com, circle.so, mightynetworks.com, docs.whop.com, support.discord.com, support.patreon.com) were blocked by the sandbox proxy, so for those I relied on search snippets of the official pages plus multiple independent secondary sources. Where sources conflict I say so. Confidence tags: **[Certain]** = official or primary source found; **[Likely]** = several consistent secondary sources; **[Guessing]** = single/unreliable source or inference.

## 1. Discord

**What/who.** Free real-time chat, voice and video platform; ~200M+ MAU and 90M+ DAU per a May 2026 Sacra equity report (third-party estimate) [Likely]. Still gaming-first in product and marketing despite a 2020 "chat for everyone" pivot and a 2024 brand refresh that re-emphasized gaming roots [Certain].

**Pricing.** Free for servers and members. Nitro ($9.99/mo) and Server Boosts are user-side. **Server Subscriptions** (creator monetization): Discord takes a 10% platform fee, but Monetization Terms add payment-processing fees on top (6% listed for desktop/browser; higher on iOS/Google Play, where Apple's 30% applies to in-app purchases), so the effective creator share is below 90% [Certain]. First payout threshold $100, then $25 [Certain].

**Critical limit for non-gaming communities:** Server Subscriptions are **US-only** (server owner must be US-based). Discord's own Creator Revenue FAQ (~mid-2025) says they "will not be available outside of the United States" and no newer source shows wider rollout [Certain, as of latest official statement]. This pushes international creators to Whop/LaunchPass-style third-party paywalls.

**Live video/streaming.** Yes: voice channels, video calls, Go Live screen share (up to 50 viewers) [Likely]; Stage channels for audio events up to 10,000 listeners, with video in Stages capped around 50 (more with boost tiers) [Certain, from Discord docs/blog, though the blog is from 2023].

**Accountability features.** None native. Check-ins, streaks, leaderboards and challenges require third-party bots (PeakBot etc.) or manual channel conventions [Certain]. Bot vendors' own marketing admits "most dead fitness servers died the same way... nothing tracked whether they came back."

**How fitness/finance/running communities use it.** Directory data (Hive Index, Oct 2026) shows Discord is the most common platform for financial-independence communities (ahead of Skool and Facebook) and fitness communities; typical structure is daily check-in channels, goal-sharing, form-feedback channels and role-gating; paid running-coaching programs use Discord for "all chatter, socials and educational tools" while training is delivered via a separate app [Likely]. Public running Discords exist but are small and hobbyist.

**Weaknesses/complaints.** Chaotic for structured programs ("considered chaotic for business courses"); DM spam on joining public servers; no native progress tracking; US-only monetization; gaming-flavored UI and onboarding; no white-label; no built-in events RSVP beyond Scheduled Events [Likely].

**2025–2026 moves.** CEO change: Jason Citron stepped down April 2025, replaced by ex-Activision exec Humam Sakhnini, widely read as IPO preparation [Certain]. **Ads:** Quests (opt-in sponsored game tasks) expanded; **Orbs** reward currency launched globally July 14, 2025; Video Quests and Arena Quests (Oct 2025) added; Kantar partnership for ad measurement; ad revenue estimated at only ~6–8% of revenue [Certain for launches; Guessing for revenue share]. **IPO:** confidentially filed a draft S-1 on Jan 6, 2026 (Goldman Sachs, JPMorgan); March 2026 target missed; no public S-1 as of early Oct 2026; secondary-market valuation ~$8.5B vs $15B 2021 round; base case now late 2026–2027 and Bloomberg has reported it may not proceed [Certain for filing; Likely for valuation/timing]. Xbox Game Pass/Nitro bundle partnership [Certain]. **Net:** Discord's strategic energy is going to gaming ads and the IPO story, not creator monetization for non-gaming niches.

## 2. Skool

**What/who.** Community + courses + calendar + gamification in one deliberately simple product. Founded by Sam Ovens; Alex Hormozi is co-owner and public face (Hormozi joined/invested in 2024; the "$100M investment" and "$1B+ valuation" figures circulating online are unsourced and Skool has never published a valuation) [Certain on involvement; Guessing on dollar figures]. Targets coaches, course creators and masterminds; strongly represented in business/"make money" niches, with growing fitness and personal-finance groups (e.g., Female Finance Collective's $5/mo debt-payoff group; "Mikey's Money House" $8/mo).

**Pricing (creator-side).** Two plans per Skool's pricing page and Help Center: **Hobby $9/mo** with **10% + $0.30 per transaction**; **Pro $99/mo** with **2.9% + $0.30** up to $899 per transaction and 3.9% + $0.30 above. Hobby added July 8, 2025 after six years of a single $99 plan [Certain]. Payments are Skool-native (Stripe underneath). Member-side: whatever the creator charges; no app-store surcharge noted.

**Growth numbers.** Estimates diverge: GetLatka ~$26.6M ARR (July 2026); CompWorth/CB Insights ~$49.8M (2026); "174k+ community creators" and "15M users" claims appear only in SEO blogs [Guessing]. "Skool Games" (Hormozi's monthly growth competition launched 2024) — some reports say paused; unconfirmed [Guessing].

**Live video.** Yes, as of 2025: **Skool Call**, native video/audio calls launchable from the calendar, 1080p recordings, no breakout rooms or webinar/registration features; Zoom/Meet still selectable; secondary sources claim up to 10,000 attendees [Likely]. Several 2026 reviews still say Skool lacks native video, so the feature is recent.

**Accountability features.** Gamification is Skool's core: points for posts/comments/likes, 9 levels (up to 33,015 pts), public leaderboard, level-gated content. Scoring logic is fixed and rewards *posting*, not *doing the thing* (no check-ins, no streaks tied to real-world actions, no stakes) [Certain]. Calendar/events with RSVP-style attendance exist [Certain].

**Weaknesses.** Thin course tooling (no quizzes/certificates), rigid branding, one feed per community, no multi-space architecture, weak for agencies running many groups, no real-time chat channels (forum-style feed + DMs), no white-label app, highest entry-tier fee in the category (10%) [Likely].

**2025–2026 moves.** $9 Hobby tier (July 2025), native Skool Call, continued Hormozi-driven marketing [Certain/Likely].

## 3. Circle.so

**What/who.** Mid-market all-in-one (spaces, courses, events, payments, email hub, live). Targets professional creators, coaches and brands wanting a branded hub.

**Pricing.** Professional **$89/mo annual** (~$99–129 monthly), Business **$199/mo annual** ($219 monthly), Circle Plus custom (~$360+/mo per secondary sources). Platform fee on paid memberships: **2% / 1% / 0.5%** on top of Stripe (Circle help center) [Certain]. No free plan; 14-day trial.

**Live.** Native Live Streams and Live Rooms: attendee caps 100 (Pro), 200 (Business), flexible on Plus; add-on $20/mo per 100 attendees; ~10 live hours/month included with $50/10hr overage; Live Rooms ~15 participants [Likely; official page metadata is old].

**Accountability features.** Gamification (points/levels/leaderboards) and "spaces" that can host challenges manually; no native check-ins, streaks or stakes [Likely].

**Weaknesses.** Cost at scale; stacked fees; live caps low for mid-sized groups; AI agents and branded app gated to Plus; multiple reviewers cite complexity for small groups [Likely].

**2025–2026 moves.** Circle AI (credits per plan), autonomous "AI Agents" on Plus, Email Hub, branded mobile app on Plus [Likely]. No major pricing shock found.

## 4. Mighty Networks

**What/who.** All-in-one with strongest native livestreaming of the course/community platforms; targets creators, memberships and branded-app customers (Mighty Pro).

**Pricing.** Launch **$79/mo annual / $95 monthly** (2% fee), Scale **$179 / $215** (1%), Growth **$354 / $425** (0.5%), Mighty Pro custom; fees stack on Stripe; iOS purchases skip the Mighty fee but incur Apple's 15% [Certain, from Mighty docs]. 2026 restructure killed the $49 entry plan [Likely].

**Live.** Native: 20 hrs/100 viewers (Launch), 30/200 (Scale), 40/1,000 (Growth), up to 10,000 viewers for extra fee [Certain].

**Accountability features.** Native **Challenges** space type (time-bound, structured), **Habit Tracker** built as a course-space checklist with daily/weekly/monthly reset, gamification, and "streaks" that count *daily app visits*, not habit completions [Certain, from Mighty docs]. Closest incumbent to native accountability, but still content-checklist based, no stakes, no wearables integration.

**Weaknesses.** Basic analytics, no certificates, pricing climbed in 2026, Co-Host AI is strategy help not member-facing [Likely].

## 5. Whop

**What/who.** Marketplace + storefront + modular "apps" (Chat, Forums, Events, Video Calls, Livestreaming, Courses) for selling digital access; dominant in trading/reselling/Discord-paywall niches, expanding to general creators.

**Pricing.** No monthly fee. **2.7% + $0.30** domestic cards; +1.5% international; +1% FX; +0.5% when subscription billing enabled; +0.8% multi-provider routing; ACH 1.5% capped $5 [Certain, Whop blog/fee page]. A separate **3% "platform fee" on Discord/Telegram/TradingView-gated products** was on the archived fee page through Dec 2025 and absent by Feb 2026; competitor blogs still cite it. **Unresolved** — effective all-in rates reported at ~4–6.5% [Likely for base; Guessing for 3%].

**Live.** Native Livestreaming app (camera/screen, OBS, chat, reactions, raise-hand multi-speaker, auto-recordings) described by competitors as "basic"; Video Calls app; Chat app "like Discord behind a paywall" [Certain for existence; Likely for quality].

**Accountability features.** None native; apps ecosystem could in theory host one [Certain].

**Weaknesses.** Spam, marketplace reputation skewed toward "make money" products, community tools rated ~3.2/5, fee opacity [Likely].

**2025–2026 moves.** **$200M strategic investment from Tether at $1.6B valuation (Feb 2026)**; ~$2.67B cumulative GMV, ~20M users; Whop Finance (yield on balances, March 2026); international expansion and AI tools [Certain]. Whop is the best-funded, fastest-moving competitor and the most natural "Discord replacement" for paid communities.

## 6. Geneva

**Status: gone as a standalone.** Acquired by Bumble (announced May 2024, closed July 2024, per Bumble 10-Qs); Bumble disclosed Geneva generated **no revenue** through June 30, 2025; the Geneva app was shut down in September 2025 when Bumble For Friends relaunched on Geneva's technology, migrating groups and messages into BFF; BFF Groups broadened in Feb 2026 [Certain]. Lesson: a free, Discord-like, non-gaming group chat with no monetization did not survive independently; its tech now serves friendship/local-groups, not creator memberships.

## 7. Patreon

**What/who.** Membership platform for creators (podcasts, video, art); community is secondary to content delivery.

**Pricing.** New creators publishing after **Aug 4, 2025 pay a flat 10%** platform fee (Pro 8% / Premium 12% consolidated; legacy 5%/8% rates grandfathered); plus processing ~2.9% + $0.30, FX ~2.5%, payout fees; a 3% add-on for former-Premium tools reported [Certain for 10%; Likely for add-on]. iOS in-app purchases carry Apple's fee passed to fans.

**Live/chat.** Community Chats (Discord-like group chats, since Sept 2023; tier-gated, moderators, web + mobile) [Certain]. **Native livestreaming** tested April 2025, rolled out summer 2025: free or paid-only streams, chat, moderators, OBS/Streamlabs, 24/7 streaming, paid recordings, no native co-hosting; **Crowdcast integration ends Oct 28, 2026** [Certain].

**Accountability features.** None [Certain].

**2025–2026 moves.** Fee simplification; merged creator/fan profiles (Nov 2025, poorly received); Aug 2026 launch of 30 features incl. short-form Clips, topic "Niches" communities and new discovery; anti-AI-scraping and moderation tools [Certain]. Patreon is converging on "content + chat + live" but has no interest in goal tracking.

## 8. Kajabi / Teachable (brief)

**Kajabi.** Basic $179/mo ($143 annual), Growth $249 ($199), Pro $499 ($399), raised Jan 2026; 1 community on Basic/Growth, 3 on Pro; Live Rooms up to 200 participants; community has threads, chat, challenges, points/leaderboards (one reviewer disputes gamification), events with RSVP; 0% platform fee [Likely]. Expensive for a pure community.

**Teachable.** Starter $39/mo (7.5% fee), Builder $89, Growth $189, custom tier; **Community feature is now in maintenance mode: new schools cannot add one, no new features or bug fixes; Teachable explicitly recommends Discord or Slack instead** (support article ~Aug 2026) [Certain]. Not a competitor for community.

## 9. Running: Strava Clubs, Nike Run Club, Runna

**Strava.** $11.99/mo or $79.99/yr; Family $139.99/yr; Strava+Runna bundle $149.99/yr [Likely]. ~90M+ members. **Clubs:** free to create; weekly leaderboard by distance (single-sport) or time (multisport), resets Sunday; club posts/events; July 2026 added an **Events tab in Groups, refreshed Club Organizer Hub, and race discovery via Runna's database** [Certain]. **Group Challenges** (private, friends): Most Activity / Fastest Effort / Longest Activity leaderboards or a collective Group Goal; **subscriber-only to create; mobile-only** [Certain]. Limits: challenges count only Strava-logged activity, mostly distance/time; organizers can't set custom goals or end dates; no chat beyond comments; no payments; club API endpoints were **removed Sept 1, 2026** and API now requires a subscription, which hurts third parties building club tooling on Strava [Certain]. Acquired **Runna** (April/May 2025) and Breakaway; valuation ~$2.2B [Certain].

**Nike Run Club.** Free; challenges with friends and global weekly/monthly challenges, badges/trophies for streaks and PBs, live run-sharing link; no clubs/groups product, no chat, no payments; no notable 2025–2026 community release found [Likely].

**Runna.** $19.99/mo or $119.99/yr, 7-day trial, no free tier; coaching plans; two-way Strava sync; **no community/club feature of its own** — social layer is Strava [Likely].

**Takeaway:** the running incumbents own *activity data and leaderboards* but have no real-time community, no monetization for club organizers, and Strava is closing its club API.

## 10. Pickleball: PicklePlay, Pickleheads, DUPR, Pickleball Brackets

**Pickleheads.** Free for players; Plus $19.99/yr; **Pro (organizers) $249.90/yr** (10 round-robins/mo, payment collection, waitlists, DUPR score submission); Ultra (facilities) $749.90/yr unlimited [Certain, official pricing page]. Court finder, sessions, group messaging. No live video, no streaks/challenges, no memberships beyond event fees.

**PicklePlay.** Free app; Pro membership $1.99/mo or $19.99/yr (family accounts, calendar sync); club/league management, round robins, skill brackets, rankings, scheduling, dues/event payments; club-tier pricing not published (a $199/mo figure circulates; unverified). Note pickleplay.com/clubs is UTR Sports software ($1,100–$3,100/yr), a different product [Likely for app; Guessing for club pricing].

**DUPR.** Rating is free; **DUPR+ $19.99/yr** (earlier $3.99/mo or $29.99/yr; sources conflict) [Likely]. Club tools: "6,000+ clubs," results submission, event-software partners; ladders/leagues are run by clubs on partner software (CourtReserve $159–549/mo, PlayByPoint, etc.) not natively [Likely].

**Pickleball Brackets / PickleballTournaments.com.** Tournament registration; player service fee was $5/event (max $10) in 2024; for tournaments opening after **Aug 3, 2026 it is $5 + 7.99% of checkout**; $25 setup [Likely].

**Takeaway:** pickleball software is scheduling/ratings/registration oriented, fragmented, and has no chat/live/accountability layer. Ladder play is a ritual *desperately* run through WhatsApp/text/spreadsheets plus one of these apps.

## 12. Facebook Groups

Why people leave: text/image post reach has collapsed as Meta's feed favors short video; "rented audience" with no member export, arbitrary restrictions/bans; no native payments for groups (Subscription Groups was discontinued); algorithmic feed vs Skool's chronological one; no gamification. Creators keep FB as top-of-funnel and move paying members to Skool/Discord; anecdotal 30–50% drop-off during migration [Likely; sources are mostly vendor blogs]. Facebook Groups remain the default for local running clubs and pickleball groups because they are free and everyone already has an account.

## 13. Telegram / WhatsApp Communities (brief)

**Telegram.** Paid channel subscriptions via Stars (30-day period), paid posts, paid reactions; creators receive 100% of Stars but app-store purchase of Stars costs ~30% on mobile; ad revenue share 50% at 1,000+ subscribers; Stars withdraw via TON/Fragment with 21-day hold; no storefront/CRM [Likely]. Strong in crypto/trading/international markets; weak trust for consumer wellness.

**WhatsApp Communities.** Up to **2,000 members per community** (across up to 100 groups of 1,024), 20 community admins, admin-only Announcements group, created from consumer WhatsApp not Business [Certain, WhatsApp FAQ]. No payments, no video rooms beyond group calls (32), no roles/gating. The 2,000 cap is a hard ceiling that pushes successful clubs elsewhere.

## Gaps analysis: is there a real gap?

**Honest headline: the gap exists, but it is narrower and more specific than "nobody does accountability." Each ingredient exists somewhere; no one combines them, and the incumbents best positioned to combine them (Mighty, Skool, Whop, Strava) each have a structural reason not to.**

**1. Verified "did you actually do it" check-ins inside a paid community — genuinely absent.** [Certain]
Skool's leaderboard rewards posting, not training. Mighty's habit tracker is a self-ticked checklist and its "streaks" count logins. Circle/Whop/Patreon/Discord have nothing. The apps that *do* verify (Strava, WHOOP, StepBet via wearables; DUPR via match results) have no community monetization. Nobody lets an organizer say "this challenge counts if Strava/Apple Health/WHOOP/DUPR shows it" and then gate roles, leaderboards or refunds on that.

**2. Group stakes with an organizer in the loop — absent.** [Certain]
StickK and Beeminder are solo; DietBet/StepBet are stranger pools run by the vendor with a 10–25% rake. No community platform offers organizer-run stakes (deposit refunded on completion, forfeit to pot/charity) with the obvious compliance work done (not gambling, not an MSB). This is a real moat opportunity but also a real regulatory/ops burden, and WayBetter's apparent stagnation (StepBet sold, RunBet reportedly gone) suggests stakes alone are not a growth business. Stakes should be a feature, not the product.

**3. Live sessions as *rituals* rather than webinars — partial gap.** [Likely]
Every platform treats live as a broadcast (Mighty, Circle, Patreon, Whop) or a call (Skool Call). None natively supports the formats accountability groups actually run: recurring 15-minute morning check-in rooms, body-doubling rooms with attendance tracked (Focusmate/FLOWN do this but without a community around it), or "run together, then talk" audio rooms. Discord Stage/voice is closest and is free, which is why fitness groups tolerate its chaos.

**4. Sport-specific structure (ladders, round robins, ratings) + community + payments — fragmented.** [Certain]
Pickleball clubs run Pickleheads/PicklePlay for scheduling, DUPR for ratings, WhatsApp for chat, Venmo/Stripe links for dues. Running clubs run Strava for data, Facebook/Discord for chat, and a Google Form for events. Strava removing club API endpoints (Sept 2026) and gating Group Challenges behind subscription makes building *on* Strava riskier and makes an independent hub more valuable, but it also means activity verification must come via Apple Health/Garmin/Google Fit rather than Strava's API.

**5. International creator monetization for Discord-style communities — gap created by Discord itself.** [Certain]
Discord Server Subscriptions remain US-only. Whop fills this for English-speaking "make money" niches; nobody fills it for fitness/wellness niches that find Whop's marketplace off-brand.

**6. Where the gap is weaker than hoped:**
- **"Discord but nicer for non-gamers" is not a gap** — Geneva tried exactly that, made no revenue, and was absorbed into Bumble BFF. Free chat without an owner economy does not survive. [Certain]
- **Gamification is commoditized.** Skool, Circle, Mighty and Kajabi all have points/levels/leaderboards. Shipping another leaderboard is table stakes, not differentiation. [Certain]
- **Mighty Networks is one product cycle away** from most of this: it already has Challenges, habit checklists, strong livestream caps and white-label apps. Its weaknesses are price ($95+/mo) and a content-first mental model, not missing primitives. [Likely]
- **Whop has the money ($200M, $1.6B valuation) and an app platform** to add an "Accountability app" quickly if a niche proves out. Speed to a defensible data/network position matters more than feature breadth. [Likely]
- **Member willingness to pay is proven but modest** in finance/fitness Skool groups ($5–$10/mo examples) versus business masterminds ($50–$500/mo). Unit economics should assume low ARPU and many small groups, which argues for a flat low creator fee plus a small take, not a 10% Skool-style tax.

**Net assessment.** There is a defensible wedge: *verified check-ins + organizer-run challenges/stakes + lightweight recurring live rooms + payments, with wearable/sport-data connectors, priced for small paid groups and available outside the US.* The wedge is real because every incumbent treats accountability as content (courses) or as engagement (posting points) rather than as verified behavior. The risk is that it is a feature set rather than a category, and that Mighty or Whop could copy it within 6–12 months of it working. Sustainable advantage will have to come from sport/habit data integrations, organizer tooling for the specific rituals (ladders, cohorts, challenges), and network effects among organizers in a few beachhead verticals (pickleball ladders and running clubs look most underserved by chat-capable, payment-capable tools).
