## Rules for the wellness conversation
1. **Ask freely, but don't diagnose.** Lifestyle questions (energy, sleep, stress, diet) are fair game. If they mention a condition, medication, or pregnancy, tick the safety check and recommend a doctor check before any supplement.
2. **Use only the support statements printed in the official catalog.** Don't paraphrase them into stronger claims. "Supports restful sleep" ≠ "fixes your insomnia".
3. **Weight:** never promise pounds, timelines, or "melt fat" outcomes.
4. **Medications, pregnancy, chronic illness:** "Check with your doctor before adding a supplement." Then move on.
5. **Habits first.** If they sleep 5 hours and drink 4 energy drinks a day, say so before suggesting a product. Recommending a product without the habit change won't work, and they'll blame the product.

## Weak spots in the portfolio for this approach
- **Price.** Nutrilite and Artistry are premium-priced. A client whose #1 priority is "saving money" will often be better served by the home-care concentrates (cost per use) than by supplements. The app's scoring ties frustrations to their stated priorities for this reason.
- **Big-ticket items (eSpring, Atmosphere, iCook).** These are hard sells at a second meeting. Only recommend them when the annual cost of what they're replacing (e.g. bottled water) makes the math obvious.
